How I work

From threat model to honest results

01

Model the attacker

Start from what can go wrong: who holds valid keys, what they can forge, and what stays out of scope.

  • Threat model first
  • Named attack scenarios
  • Explicit assumptions
02

Build and measure

Implement every component, then measure it against real baselines under the same tuning and seeds.

  • Separate tuning and test seeds
  • Rebuilt baselines
  • Wall-clock timings
03

Report the limits

Ablations and negative results go in the paper, along with what the numbers do not prove.

  • Ablation studies
  • Negative results kept
  • Scope stated plainly

About

Background

I'm a cybersecurity graduate student at SRM University-AP, based in Hyderabad. My work sits where cryptography meets messy real-world systems: sensors that lie with valid signatures, IDS engines that slow down as rule sets grow, and ledgers that have to stay cheap at IoT data rates.

Since September 2026 I've been interning as a Cybersecurity Analyst at XSignOn. Before the M.Tech I interned at the Defence Research and Development Laboratory (DRDL), Hyderabad, doing vulnerability assessment. That habit of asking "what breaks this?" carries into my research.

Interests: network security, intrusion detection, digital forensics, zero-trust architectures for IoT, post-quantum cryptography and blockchain-based security.

Quick answers

FAQ

What kind of opportunities are you looking for?

I'm currently a Cybersecurity Analyst intern at XSignOn. I'm also open to research collaborations and security engineering roles, especially post-quantum migration, intrusion detection, and IoT or vehicular security.

What is your M.Tech thesis about?

A behavioral trust layer for post-quantum, DAG-based vehicular sensor networks. Signatures prove who sent data; the trust layer decides whether to believe it, and every trust decision is signed with ML-DSA-65 into an audit ledger.

Can I see the code behind the papers?

Yes. The trust layer, the IoT framework and the IDS study each have a public repository on GitHub, linked from the research cards and the repositories section.

Are your results from real systems or simulations?

Mostly simulations and public datasets (KDD Cup 99, NSL-KDD, CICIDS2017, CUB-200-2011). Each paper says which numbers are measured and which are modeled, and lists real-world testing as future work.
